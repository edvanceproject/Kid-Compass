# Interest ladders

A ladder shows a parent where an interest can lead, one rung at a time, so the opportunity map is a path rather than a random list. Show the ladder for the child's top one or two sparks, mark where the child is now, and name the **next** rung — the one worth doing this year.

Two reminders when presenting a ladder:

- Most children should spend most of their time on the lower rungs, enjoying them. The top rungs are there to show the path exists, not to set a target.
- Programmes marked with a registry id are in `registry/national/`; check the file for eligibility and dates. Others are typical rungs to find through search.

## The non-competitive growth path

Every interest also has a path that involves no contests at all. Use it for "fun only" families, for shy children, and as a counterweight in any plan:

1. **Try** — a taster: a workshop, a library book, a free app, a museum visit
2. **Make** — regular making at home or in a club, at the child's own pace
3. **Finish** — complete one thing: a comic, a model, a short film, a garden bed
4. **Share** — with family and friends; a zine, a home exhibition, a school magazine page
5. **Show** — a school display, a community exhibition, a festival stall, supervised online sharing
6. **Collaborate or teach** — make something with others, help a younger child, run a club

The child moves up by finishing and sharing things, not by winning. For online sharing, mention a pen name, parent supervision and the platform's minimum age and under-18 rules.

### Comics and illustration (non-competitive)

Draw for fun → copy and study favourite artists → finish a one-page or 8-page comic → fold it into a zine for friends → a page in the school magazine or a supervised webcomic → visit comic and zine fests to see working artists → share a table at a zine fest with a friend, when she wants to.

## Rung pattern (with competitions)

1. **Explore** — free or low-cost tasters: museum and planetarium programmes, library sessions, hobby clubs, school clubs.
2. **Show** — school-level fairs, exhibitions, contests, inter-house events.
3. **Compete locally** — inter-school, city or district events.
4. **State and national** — government or recognised national programmes and olympiads.
5. **International** — selections that run through the national level.

## Science and innovation

1. Planetarium or science museum workshops, home experiments, school science club
2. School science fair or exhibition
3. District or regional level: the first rounds of the national programmes below; for CBSE schools, `cbse-science-exhibition` (regional → national)
4. National programmes:
   - `inspire-manak` — original idea; the **school** nominates
   - `ncsc` — two-student research project with a guide teacher; district → state → national
   - `school-innovation-marathon` — team innovation challenge
   - `iris-national-fair` — independent research projects, for older students
   - `nsejs` — written exam, Class 10 and below, first stage of the junior science olympiad
5. International: the national programmes above select students for international events (see each file's notes)

## Maths

1. Puzzles, maths clubs, strategy games
2. School olympiads and maths days
3. Inter-school maths contests
4. `ioqm` — the first stage of India's official maths olympiad pathway, Classes 8–12
5. International Mathematical Olympiad, selected through the national pathway

## Space

1. Planetarium shows and sky-watching events, astronomy clubs
2. School science fair projects on space
3. Space quiz and model contests
4. `isro-yuvika` — ISRO's residential young scientist programme for Class 9
5. Astronomy olympiad pathway for older students (search for the current national process)

## Coding, computational thinking and robotics

1. Unplugged puzzles, block coding, robotics workshops
2. School coding club or tech fest
3. `bebras-india` — computational thinking challenge, many age groups
4. `wro-india` — team robotics competition with a national final
5. International finals through the national round

## Art

1. Workshops at art councils and galleries, sketching outdoors
2. School and inter-school art competitions
3. City and state-level competitions run by art councils or reputable newspapers
4. `shankars-international` — a long-running international children's painting and writing competition, entered by post
5. Exhibitions of children's art; for exceptional achievement, `pm-rashtriya-bal-puraskar`

## Writing and reading

1. Library reading programmes, children's literature festivals, writing at home
2. School magazine, essay and story contests
3. Inter-school and city writing contests
4. National children's writing competitions (search), `shankars-international` (writing category)
5. Published anthologies and international children's writing prizes

## Quiz and debate

1. Quiz nights, family quizzes, reading widely
2. Inter-house quizzes and debates
3. Inter-school quiz and debate events
4. `tcs-inquizitive` — inter-school quiz for Classes 8–12, city rounds to a national final (successor to TCS IT Wiz)
5. National-level quizzes and Model UN conferences (check credibility)

## Performing arts (music, dance, theatre)

1. Children's workshops at theatres and cultural centres; listening to and watching performances
2. School annual day, music and dance events
3. Inter-school festivals; recognised graded examinations
4. State and national youth festivals and children's theatre festivals
5. National recognition such as `pm-rashtriya-bal-puraskar` for exceptional achievement

## Environment and nature

1. Nature walks, bird counts, school eco-club, gardening
2. School environment projects
3. City-level environment competitions and citizen-science events
4. `ncsc` — sustainability-themed research projects
5. National youth environment programmes (search)

## Sports and chess

1. Play for fun; neighbourhood and club play
2. School sports day, house teams
3. Inter-school and district school games
4. State and national school games, and national federation age-group events
5. National team selection through the sport's federation

Sports pathways depend on the sport and state; use search for the right federation and the current school-games calendar.

## Social impact and entrepreneurship

1. Volunteering with the family, small school projects
2. School service or business-fair events
3. City-level youth challenges
4. `school-innovation-marathon` (problem-solving for the community), recognised national youth awards
5. For exceptional work: `pm-rashtriya-bal-puraskar`
