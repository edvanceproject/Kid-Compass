# The Spark interview

The aim is to learn what the child gravitates to on their own, plus the family's practical limits, in one short exchange. Free-time behaviour predicts lasting interest better than school marks, so most questions ask what the child *does*, not what they're *good at*.

## When to skip it

If the parent's first message already gives age or class, city, and at least one concrete interest, don't run the interview. Build a first opportunity map right away and ask at most two follow-up questions at the end ("Is she happy presenting on stage, or would she rather build and show?").

## The parent version (send in one message)

Pick the 6–8 questions that matter for this family; don't send all of them. Number them so the parent can answer "1. …, 2. …".

**About the child**
1. How old is your child, and which class and board (CBSE, ICSE, State board, IB, other)?
2. When nobody tells them what to do, what do they end up doing? (Drawing, building, reading, arguing, organising friends, running around, watching how-things-work videos…)
3. What have they taken apart, collected, or asked endless questions about lately?
4. Do they light up more on a stage, in a team, or working alone on something?
5. Something they tried and disliked — and what they didn't like about it?
6. Which languages are they comfortable speaking or presenting in?

**About the family's limits**
7. Which city and area, and how far are you happy to travel for a one-off event?
8. Roughly how much per month can go to activities — free only, under ₹1,000, ₹1,000–5,000, more?
9. Which days are realistically free — weekends, weekday evenings, school holidays only?
10. How much competition does your family want: fun only, some challenge, or serious preparation?

Close with: *"If it's easier, your child can play a 2-minute 'this or that' game with me instead of you answering 2–5."*

## The "this or that" game (for the child, with the parent present)

Keep it light and quick. Present pairs; there are no wrong answers. Use 8–10 pairs, adapted to age (simpler words under 8).

- Build a robot **or** draw a comic?
- Explore a forest **or** explore a museum?
- Be on stage **or** be behind the stage making it work?
- Solve a puzzle alone **or** win a game with a team?
- Write a story **or** tell a story out loud?
- Watch the stars **or** watch a cricket match?
- Grow a plant **or** take care of a pet?
- Invent a new game **or** become the best at an old one?
- Make music **or** dance to it?
- Start a lemonade stand **or** organise a cleanliness drive?

Map choices to sparks loosely (two or three consistent picks point the same way). Then ask one open question: *"What's something you'd love to try but haven't yet?"* — children often name the thing parents didn't know about.

## Turning answers into sparks

Group what you heard into 2–4 sparks. Common ones, with the interest tags used in the registry:

| Spark | Typical signs | Registry interests |
|---|---|---|
| Builder | Takes things apart, Lego, asks how machines work | engineering, robotics, innovation |
| Scientist | Experiments, "why" questions, nature, space | science, space, environment |
| Number cruncher | Puzzles, patterns, mental maths, strategy games | maths, chess, coding |
| Coder | Games, wants to make apps, logic puzzles | coding, robotics |
| Artist | Draws everywhere, colours, crafts | art |
| Storyteller | Writes, reads a lot, invents stories | writing, reading, theatre |
| Performer | Sings, dances, acts, loves an audience | music, dance, theatre |
| Debater / quizzer | Argues, collects facts, loves trivia | debate, quiz |
| Explorer | Outdoors, animals, maps, travel | environment, sports |
| Athlete | Runs everywhere, team games | sports |
| Changemaker | Organises friends, cares about causes | social-impact, entrepreneurship |
| All-rounder | A bit of everything, no strong pull yet | all-round |

Write each spark with evidence in the parent's own words. If interests are unclear, that is useful information: lead with "taster" activities across two or three areas rather than competitions.

## The debrief (after each event)

Ask the parent to ask the child these three questions within a day or two, and to send back the answers:

1. **Loved it / it was okay / never again?**
2. **What surprised you?** (Often reveals the real spark — the robot stall at a maths fair.)
3. **Want to do more of this — the same thing, or something harder?**

Update the sparks: strengthen what was loved, drop or pause "never again", and follow the surprise. Record the event, date and any certificate under `history` in the profile.
