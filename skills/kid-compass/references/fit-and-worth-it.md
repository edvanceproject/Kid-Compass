# Fit and worth-it checks

Every candidate gets two judgements before it reaches the parent: how well it fits this child and family, and whether it's worth the time and money. Give the reason with each label in a few words, so the parent can disagree with you.

## 1. Eligibility (hard filter)

Drop the candidate if any of these fails. When a rule is unclear, keep it and say "check eligibility" rather than guessing.

- **Class**: within the programme's range. Some ranges carry conditions (e.g. Classes 11–12 science stream only).
- **Age**: compute age **as of the programme's cut-off date** (often 31 December or 1 July of the competition year), not today. An 11-year-old born in late December may still be 10 on the cut-off.
- **Board**: most national programmes are open to all boards; some (a CBSE exhibition, a state board contest) aren't.
- **Team**: if it needs a team of two or more, flag that the child needs teammates and a guide teacher where applicable.
- **Route**: who submits — parent, child, school, district office or a coach/club. A school-route programme is still eligible, but the family needs the school to act.
- **Window**: if registration has closed, move it to "Missed it?" with the next expected window.

## 2. Fit label

| Label | Meaning | Typical signs |
|---|---|---|
| **Sweet spot** | Strong match to a spark at the right level | Interest matches, eligible, manageable prep, within budget |
| **Stretch** | Worth aiming for, needs real preparation | National-level exam or selective programme; months of prep |
| **Easy win** | Low effort, low cost, likely fun | Local workshop, open exhibition, school-level contest |

Also weigh, in a few words each when relevant:
- **Distance and travel**: a one-off trip is fine; a weekly class across the city usually isn't.
- **Cost**: fee plus travel, materials and any "mandatory" kit.
- **Prep time**: hours per week and for how many weeks.
- **Language**: can the child present in the required language?
- **Competition appetite**: respect "fun only" — no contests, olympiads or judged events at all, not just fewer.
- **Social comfort**: for a shy child, prefer formats with no stage or audience, small groups (4–8), solo or one-friend activities, quiet venues and quiet times (weekday afternoons, early on a festival day). Crowded fests are fine as a visitor with a friend. Never frame shyness as a problem to fix.

A good final list mixes labels: one or two Stretch goals, a few Sweet spots, and Easy wins that keep things fun.

## 3. Worth-it check

| Label | When to use it |
|---|---|
| **⭐ Credible** | Run by a government body, a recognised academic or professional body, or a long-running reputable organisation; clear rules; fees modest relative to what's offered |
| **⚠️ Check first** | Not enough information found, a new organiser, or one or two of the warning signs below |
| **🚩 Pay-to-participate** | Several warning signs together — the main product appears to be the certificate or medal, not the experience |

Warning signs (count them, don't assume):
- Every participant receives a medal or "gold" certificate regardless of performance.
- "International" or "world" in the name, but no international round, partner bodies or past international results.
- Fees that rise at each level, or paid "certificate upgrades".
- No named organisation, address or past results on the website; only a phone number or WhatsApp.
- Pressure tactics: "last 2 seats", deadlines that keep extending.
- Mandatory paid coaching or books from the organiser.

Positive signs:
- Government, university, scientific society or long-running media/NGO organiser.
- Published rules, judging criteria, past winners and results.
- Free or low cost, or fees that clearly fund the event.
- A path to a recognised higher level (state, national, international selection).

### Checking a contest the parent only has a forward or flyer for

Parents often ask about a contest they saw in a WhatsApp forward, a school flyer or an Instagram ad. Check it before judging:

1. Search the exact name in quotes, then the name with "olympiad", "organiser" or "results", and the website or phone number from the forward if there is one.
2. Look for: an official website, a named organisation with an address, published rules and syllabus, past results or winners, a real higher round (national/international) with dates and a venue, and the fee on the organiser's own page.
3. Treat these as warning signs too:
   - **No official website or named organiser can be found** for something that charges a fee.
   - **A name that echoes a well-known programme** but doesn't match it (borrowed credibility) — say what the real programme is.
4. When the warning signs come only from the forward (not from an organiser page you could open), say so in the label: *"🚩 Pay-to-participate — based on what the forward says; I couldn't find an official site"*. Dates and fees from a forward are marked *"per the forward, unverified"*.
5. Give the parent a three-point checklist they can use themselves on the next forward: who runs it, does everyone get a medal, is there a real next round.

Describe what you found, factually: *"⚠️ Check first — ₹1,800 entry, every participant gets a medal per their FAQ, and I couldn't find an international round."* Never call an organiser a fraud; let the parent decide.

## 4. Balance check

Before presenting, add up what you've suggested for the next month: hours of prep and events, and money (fees plus travel). Compare with what the family said they can manage. If it's over, cut Stretch items first and keep at least one Easy win. If the plan is all competitions, swap one for a non-competitive experience (a workshop, a museum day, a nature walk).
