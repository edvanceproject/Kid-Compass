# School nudge notes

Several of the best Indian programmes can only be entered through the school: the principal or a nominated teacher submits the entry (INSPIRE-MANAK is the clearest example). Parents often don't know, and schools are busy, so a short, polite, specific note to the class teacher or principal makes a real difference.

## How to write one

- Short: 5–7 lines. Teachers read these on their phone.
- Specific: programme name, what the school needs to do, the deadline, and the official link.
- Helpful, not demanding: offer what the family will do (help the child prepare the idea, find a teammate).
- Correct channel: the school diary, the school's parent app or email, or the class teacher's WhatsApp if that's how the school works. Don't suggest going over the teacher's head first.
- Leave placeholders in square brackets for anything you don't know: [Teacher's name], [child's name], [class and section]. Don't fill in the school name or child's full name yourself — the parent does.
- Send it early: at least 2–3 weeks before the school's internal deadline, which is usually earlier than the official one.

## English

> Dear [Teacher's name],
>
> I hope you're well. [Child's name] in [class and section] is very interested in [programme name], run by [organiser]. Entries are submitted by the school, and this year's window closes on [deadline] ([official link]).
>
> Could the school consider nominating students this year? [Child's name] is happy to prepare an idea, and we'll support from home.
>
> Thank you for your time.
> [Parent's name]

## Other languages

Write in the same spirit: greeting, the child's interest, what the school needs to do and by when, the official link, an offer of support, thanks. Keep the respectful register the language expects for addressing a teacher. Tell the parent to read it over before sending, especially in a language you're less sure of.

## Variants

- **Team programmes** (e.g. NCSC needs a guide teacher): ask whether a teacher could guide a team, and mention that the child is looking for a teammate.
- **No school route needed** but the school could help (exam centres, school-level rounds): ask whether the school is registering as a centre this year.
- **Several families together**: a note from a few parents asking the school to register a group is often more effective. Offer a version without any child's name for a parents' group.
