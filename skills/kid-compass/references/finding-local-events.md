# Finding local events

The registry covers recurring national programmes. Local, one-off events — a weekend workshop, a science exhibition, a children's theatre festival — change every month and have to be found live. This is where most "this weekend" items come from.

## 1. Start with the city pack

If `registry/cities/<city>.yaml` exists, open the `events_url` of the venues that match the child's sparks. Long-running venues (science museums, planetariums, children's theatres, bal bhavans, art councils) are the most reliable sources of good, low-cost children's programmes. Check what's listed for the next 4–8 weeks.

If there's no city pack, find the equivalent venues with a search such as `science museum children workshop <city>` or `bal bhavan <city>`. (Only if the user seems keen to help other parents, mention that the open-source project welcomes city packs.)

## 2. Search, by spark, with the month

Use short queries with the city, the interest, the audience and the month and year. Run one query per spark rather than one query for everything. Examples:

- `kids science workshop Bengaluru November 2026`
- `children art competition Pune December 2026`
- `inter school quiz Chennai 2026`
- `robotics competition school students Hyderabad 2026`
- `children's theatre festival Mumbai 2026`
- `summer camp kids Bengaluru 2027` (for the planning season, usually Feb–Apr)
- Regional languages work well for local events: `ಮಕ್ಕಳ ಕಾರ್ಯಾಗಾರ ಬೆಂಗಳೂರು`, `बच्चों के लिए प्रतियोगिता जयपुर`.

For "fun only" families, leave "competition" out of the query and search for workshops, clubs, festivals and exhibitions instead.

**Niche interests** (comics, anime, origami, astronomy, birding, coding games) often have no "kids' event" pages. Try the communities around the interest:
- cultural institutes and consulates (Japan Foundation and Japanese consulates for anime and manga culture, Alliance Française, Goethe-Institut / Max Mueller Bhavan, British Council libraries)
- comic cons, zine fests, maker fairs, book fairs and literature festivals with children's tracks
- independent bookshops, libraries and hobby shops that host events
- clubs and societies (astronomy clubs, birding groups, chess clubs)

If nothing local and confirmable turns up, say so and suggest at-home or online tasters instead of padding the list.

Never put the child's name, school or exact address into a query.

Useful source types: venue websites, government and science department notices (state councils for science and technology, education department circulars), school board circulars (CBSE, state boards), reputable newspapers' city pages, and event listing platforms. Listing platforms are fine for discovery but confirm on the organiser's own page.

## 3. Confirm before recommending

Open the organiser's own page (not just a search snippet or a listing aggregator) and confirm:

- date, time and venue
- age or class range
- fee and what it includes
- how to register, and the registration deadline

If you can't confirm a detail, keep the event only if it's otherwise strong, and mark it: *"date from the listing; not confirmed on the organiser's site"*.

Some government and venue sites block automated reading or don't load. When that happens, say so plainly, give the link, and suggest the parent phone or check in person — don't present the details as confirmed. A standing venue that's always worth a visit (a science museum's galleries) is still a fine "this month" item even without a dated event.

## 4. What makes a good local pick

- Hands-on and age-appropriate.
- Low cost, or free, especially for a first "taster".
- Within the family's travel radius and on their free days.
- Run by a venue or organisation with a track record (see the worth-it check).

Prefer 2–4 strong local picks over a long list.
